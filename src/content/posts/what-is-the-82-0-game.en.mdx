If you could choose five players from all of NBA history for one full regular season, who would make your lineup?

Michael Jordan or Kobe Bryant? Should LeBron James occupy a forward spot or run the offense? At center, would you take Shaquille O'Neal, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, or the statistically overwhelming Wilt Chamberlain?

Questions like these used to live in forums, podcasts, and group chats. The browser game **82-0**, which went viral in 2026, turned the endless all-time-five debate into a roster-building challenge that takes minutes to finish and is remarkably easy to replay.

## What is 82-0?

82-0 is a lightweight game built around historical NBA players. Under a set of random constraints, you select five players, assemble a lineup, and receive a projected record across a full NBA regular season.

The name states the ultimate goal: **82 wins and zero losses**.

An NBA team currently plays 82 regular-season games. No team has ever completed that schedule undefeated. The closest was the 2015–16 Golden State Warriors, who finished 73-9 and passed the Chicago Bulls' previous 72-win record. The [NBA's official schedule release](https://pr.nba.com/2025-26-nba-regular-season-schedule/) confirms the 82-game format, while the league's [review of the Warriors' record](https://jr.nba.com/warriors-chase-history-2/) identifies 73-9 as the best regular-season record in NBA history.

That makes 82-0 less a realistic forecast than a deliberately near-impossible fantasy target.

## How does the game work?

The basic loop is simple.

Each round assigns you an NBA franchise and a decade — for example, the Warriors in the 1960s or the Bulls in the 1990s. You select one player who represented that franchise during that period, add him to your five-man lineup, and repeat until the roster is complete.

You receive one team re-spin and one decade re-spin. Those two choices create the game's main resource-management decision: spend them early and you may regret a worse draw later; hold them forever and you may waste a chance to repair the roster.

When the lineup is complete, the game aggregates categories including points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks into a **Strength Rating**, then runs that total through a non-linear projection to produce an 82-game record. [ESPN's report on the game](https://abc7chicago.com/post/82-0-pacers-haliburton-bucks-more-get-viral-game/19241341/) describes this model, and the game's [official instructions](https://www.82-0.com/how-to-play) list the same five core statistics.

As of July 2026, the website offers modes including Classic, Hoop IQ, and 1v1. Classic displays player statistics; Hoop IQ hides them and relies more heavily on memory; 1v1 pits two drafted lineups against each other. A mobile version operated by Vaulty Studios Inc. is also available. These details can be verified on the [official 82-0 website](https://www.82-0.com/) and its [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/82-0-com/id6780195090).

## Why did such a simple game go viral?

82-0 was created by software engineer and NBA fan Roy Saar. According to an interview with publishing partner PlayVault, it began as a project for friends and family. After the game spread rapidly in early June 2026, Saar approached PlayVault to help scale it. The story is documented in [DNVR Sports' interview with the team](https://thednvr.com/nuggets/82-0-nba-game-starting-lineup-viral-interview/).

Its appeal rests on three unusually shareable qualities.

First, almost no onboarding is required. Spin, choose a player, finish the lineup, and reveal the record. A full attempt takes only a few minutes.

Second, the game manufactures debate. Some fans value Jordan's two-way dominance; others prefer LeBron's versatility. Some insist a lineup needs rim protection, while others would stack five elite scorers. The projected record does not settle those arguments — it gives people new material for them.

Third, the result fits neatly into a screenshot. Friends can instantly understand the lineup and react: “How is that only a 77-win team?” or “Replace one player and it goes 82-0.” Sports writer Kaelen Jones described games of this kind as multiplayer experiences disguised as solo play: the draft happens alone, but the real experience unfolds in group chats and social feeds. His [report on 82-0 and participatory sports fandom](https://kaelenjones.com/p/geosports-82-0-sports-community-narrative-usc-durbin) also documents involvement from NBA players, team accounts, and television personalities.

One of the most visible examples came from Tyrese Haliburton. A lineup featuring LeBron James, Kobe Bryant, Michael Jordan, Larry Bird, and Moses Malone received a 77-5 record, prompting a frustrated social post. The Bulls, Bucks, and Heat were among the NBA team accounts that also joined the conversation.

## How should you draft for 82-0?

Based on the public rules, the game emphasizes statistical totals more than a complete model of basketball fit. The practical approach is therefore not simply to collect the five most famous names. It is to cover all five measured categories.

If the lineup already has several prolific scorers, the next pick may be more valuable as a source of rebounds, assists, steals, or blocks. More scoring alone may not move the projected record very far.

Historically versatile players tend to help because they fill several categories at once. Chamberlain is particularly powerful in this model. ESPN used the example of a 1960s Warriors draw: selecting Chamberlain immediately supplies a base of 41.5 points and 25.1 rebounds per game.

The two re-spins should also be saved for combinations that seriously limit the remaining roster. Classic mode lets you make these decisions from visible numbers; Hoop IQ tests whether you remember what a player actually produced for a particular franchise in a particular decade.

## It is not a full basketball simulator

An 82-0 result should not be treated as a rigorous prediction of historical matchups.

Real basketball includes shooting efficiency, spacing, usage, switching, pace, bench depth, coaching, injuries, and chemistry. Rules and statistical environments also differ sharply between eras. A handful of per-game categories cannot reproduce all of that.

The creators have not published the exact formula. In their interview, they called it the game's “secret sauce” and said it continues to be adjusted. We can verify which public statistics inform the result, but we cannot treat the final record as an objective basketball conclusion.

That limitation is also part of the design. 82-0 is not trying to replace a professional simulation. It compresses basketball history, randomness, and roster debate into a fast challenge. A result that feels wrong often creates another attempt — or another argument with friends.

## What 82-0 really simulates

The most interesting thing about 82-0 is not whether it can identify five players who would literally go undefeated. It is the way it turns the familiar all-time-lineup argument into something people can play, compare, and share.

In a few minutes, the player goes through a miniature front-office exercise: accept random constraints, weigh historical stars, repair statistical weaknesses, and live with a result that may defy expectations.

A real 82-0 season may never happen. In a game, though, people can keep trying to find the perfect lineup that exists only in basketball imagination.

The [73-9 game](/) brings that fantasy slightly closer to the ground. Instead of chasing a fictional undefeated opponent, you draft under a $100M salary cap and random team-season constraints to challenge the real Warriors team that finished 73-9. The addictive question is the same one that powers 82-0: “What if I had picked someone else?”
