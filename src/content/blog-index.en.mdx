## Basketball history, game strategy, and transparent development notes

The 73-9 blog supports one focused experience: building a five-player roster under a $100 million cap and testing it against the 2015-16 Golden State Warriors benchmark. Articles here are not filler around a game embed. They explain the historical reference, document how the challenge should be interpreted, and help players make better decisions on later runs.

Every article is written or reviewed for this project. Historical claims are checked against reliable sources, and game-specific explanations are based on the implementation used on 73-9.org. When an article includes an opinion—such as whether a balanced lineup is safer than a stars-and-minimums build—it is presented as strategy inside the game rather than as an objective truth about real basketball.

## What you can learn here

### Draft strategy

The wheel creates a limited market and the cap connects all five decisions. Strategy articles examine when a premium signing is worth the cost, how to estimate a safe budget for remaining positions, when a re-spin solves a real problem, and why a productive player-season can be more useful than a famous career name.

These guides do not promise a guaranteed winning lineup. The team-seasons change from run to run, so the useful skill is recognizing trade-offs rather than memorizing one list of players.

### Historical context

The number 73-9 refers to Golden State’s record-setting 2015-16 regular season. Historical articles explain why that season became the game’s target, how it compares with the 72-10 Chicago Bulls, and why a regular-season record is only one part of a “greatest team” discussion.

When we use specific records or awards, we link to official NBA material or another clearly identified source. The site’s simulation and player prices remain independent game mechanics.

### Methodology and limitations

A browser game cannot recreate every element of cross-era basketball. Box-score production, minutes, and shooting provide useful signals, but they cannot fully capture coaching, off-ball movement, defensive positioning, injuries, rule changes, or team chemistry.

Methodology articles explain what the output is useful for and what it does not claim. A simulated percentage is a consistent comparison inside the 73-9 model. It is not betting advice, a scouting grade, or proof of how a real series would finish.

### Product updates

Development notes record meaningful changes to data, pricing, game flow, performance, accessibility, privacy, and editorial content. A change log matters because a run created under one version may not be perfectly comparable with a run created after a data or balance update.

We prefer concrete notes over vague announcements. An update should explain what changed, why it changed, and what a player will notice.

## Recommended reading path

New players should start with the [complete How to Play guide](/how-to-play). It explains the wheel, roster selection, re-spins, budget management, simulation, and comparison lineup.

Next, read [How 73-9 Works](/how-it-works) for the relationship between historical data and game design. That page also documents the model’s limitations and links to official sources for the Warriors benchmark.

After completing a run, use [How to Read Your Simulation Result](/blog/how-to-read-your-simulation-result). It explains the simulated record, Warriors percentage, and the “best squad you could have signed” comparison.

Players who repeatedly run out of money should continue with [Draft Strategy Under a $100M Cap](/blog/draft-strategy-under-100m). The guide provides a practical spending framework rather than a list that becomes outdated whenever the wheel changes.

For historical background, read [Why the 73-9 Warriors Are the Benchmark](/blog/why-the-73-9-warriors-are-the-benchmark).

## How articles are prepared

A useful 73-9 article should do at least one of four things:

1. Help a player make a better decision.
2. Explain a game mechanic or limitation.
3. Add sourced historical context.
4. Document a meaningful product change.

We avoid publishing pages that only repeat a title, paraphrase another site, or target a keyword without answering a real question. Article ideas are evaluated against the game itself: would a player understand the experience better after reading this?

Historical facts are separated from game interpretation. For example, Golden State’s 73-9 record and Stephen Curry’s unanimous MVP are documented events. Whether a certain cap structure is the best way to attack the game is a strategic judgment that depends on the wheel outcomes.

Articles may be updated when the implementation or source material changes. The publication date remains visible, and significant methodological changes should also appear in a development note.

## Independence and corrections

73-9.org is an independent fan project and is not affiliated with the NBA, any team, or any player. Player and team names are used for identification, commentary, education, and simulation.

We aim to correct factual, data, and gameplay errors. A useful correction request includes the article URL, the disputed sentence or player-season, a reliable source when available, and a screenshot for interface issues.

Send corrections and article questions to [support@73-9.org](mailto:support@73-9.org). Privacy requests should use the same address and follow the instructions in the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy).

## Play, read, and compare

The best way to use the blog is alongside the game. Complete a run, note where the budget became difficult, study the comparison lineup, and then read the guide that matches the problem. Return to the [game](/) and test one change.

That cycle—draft, simulate, review, learn, and retry—is the central value of 73-9.
