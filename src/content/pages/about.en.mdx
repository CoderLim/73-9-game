## What is the 73-9 game?

The **73-9 game** is a free browser basketball strategy game at [73-9.org](https://73-9.org). It gives you a $100 million budget, five lineup positions, and a sequence of random franchise-seasons. From each team-season you may sign one player. When the lineup is complete, the 73-9 game simulates an 82-game season and estimates how often your five clears the benchmark set by the 2015-16 Golden State Warriors.

The site has one primary purpose. It is not a large game portal, a collection of embedded titles, or a feed of copied sports stories. The playable 73-9 game, guides, methodology notes, and strategy articles all support the same roster-building challenge.

You can play without an account or download. An account is optional and is used for features such as saving eligible runs to the [leaderboard](/leaderboard).

## Where the name comes from

Golden State finished the 2015-16 regular season with 73 wins and 9 losses. That record passed the 1995-96 Chicago Bulls’ 72-10 mark for the most regular-season wins in NBA history.

The number became a compact way for fans to refer to an extraordinary season. Stephen Curry won the league’s first unanimous Most Valuable Player award and made 402 three-pointers. The Warriors opened the season 24-0 and won 34 road games.

The season also contains a useful warning about what one number cannot prove. Golden State lost the 2016 NBA Finals, while the 72-10 Bulls won the 1996 championship. Regular-season dominance, playoff results, era, opponent quality, health, and style all matter in historical comparisons.

The 73-9 game does not try to settle that debate. It uses the record as a clear challenge: can you build a lineup strong enough to compete with the team that established the highest regular-season win total?

## Why make the 73-9 game a budget challenge?

A historical fantasy lineup is easy when every slot can contain an all-time superstar. It becomes more interesting when every selection has a cost.

The $100M cap forces players to make connected decisions. Spending heavily on one player can be correct, but it leaves less room for the other four positions. A cheaper player-season can become more valuable than a bigger name when it preserves the budget needed to complete a balanced roster.

The random team-season wheel adds a second constraint. You are not choosing from the full history of basketball. Each spin creates a limited market, and each run presents a new sequence of opportunities.

Together, randomness and the budget turn a list of famous names into a decision game.

## Why player-seasons matter

The 73-9 game treats a player in a specific season as the draft asset. Careers are not uniform. A player’s role, minutes, production, efficiency, teammates, and health can change significantly over time.

This means reputation alone is not enough. The displayed team-season matters. A less famous player in an unusually productive year may be a better fit than a star in a reduced role. The 73-9 game rewards reading the available season rather than drafting a career résumé.

Historical data informs the player pool, ratings, and prices, while the rules convert that data into a consistent game economy. The result is an abstraction, not an official NBA evaluation.

## What happens during a 73-9 game run

A normal run follows seven steps:

1. Start with five open positions and a $100M budget.
2. Spin for a random franchise-season.
3. Review the players available from that roster.
4. Sign one affordable player or pay for a re-spin.
5. Repeat until the lineup is complete.
6. Simulate an 82-game season and the Warriors matchup.
7. Compare your choices with a stronger legal lineup built from the same team-seasons.

The comparison lineup is especially important. It keeps the wheel outcomes constant and shows what different decisions could have produced. That makes each finished run useful even when the simulated result is poor.

A complete rules walkthrough is available on [How to play the 73-9 game](/how-to-play), and the model is described on [How the 73-9 game works](/how-it-works).

## What the project values

### A complete free experience

The core 73-9 game is playable in the browser without payment. The project may use advertising to support hosting and development, but advertising should not replace, obstruct, or appear before the main experience is available.

### Original first-party value

The game logic, interface, explanatory guides, strategy articles, and project documentation are maintained for this site. External historical facts are used as sources, not copied as substitute content.

### Clear limitations

Cross-era basketball simulations cannot reproduce every tactical, physical, and historical variable. Results are entertainment and a way to compare decisions inside the same model. They are not betting advice or definitive claims about real players and teams.

### User control

Guests can play without creating an account. When optional analytics, advertising, or account features process data, the site explains those practices in the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy) and [Cookie Policy](/cookies).

### Continuous correction

Historical datasets and game balancing can contain mistakes. Clear bug reports and data corrections are welcome at [support@73-9.org](mailto:support@73-9.org).
