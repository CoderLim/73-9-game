## The short version

73-9 turns the NBA’s best regular-season record into a bankroll puzzle. A wheel lands on random franchise seasons; you sign one player per stop; prices map historical production to a modern salary-cap scale; an 82-game sim reports how often your five beats the 2015-16 Warriors. Results are entertainment—not predictions or betting advice.

For the click-by-click flow, see [How to play](/how-to-play).

## Where the $100M budget comes from

Each player is priced by what that season’s production would be worth against the 2026 salary cap. A prime Jokic or a 33-9-9 Luka can devour most of your cash in one signing. Land a megastar and the other four spots become role players, veterans, and the occasional minimum.

Five max contracts would cost nearly half a billion; you have one hundred million. That tension is the point. The best players make you the best team—and instantly make you poor. Winning means hunting seasons where someone was quietly elite and underpriced, then stitching five value pieces together before the money runs out.

## How player ratings work

Each signed player carries a rating built from real per-game scoring, playmaking, rebounding, and shooting efficiency, weighted by minutes played. Historical box scores feed the pool; ratings and prices create the economy.

Add the five ratings and you get a team-strength number. That number drives the season sim and the head-to-head projection against the 2015-16 Warriors—a club sitting near the top 0.1% of historical team strength.

## How the simulation decides if you win

A merely good five loses to that bar most nights. To win consistently you need star power five deep—which the budget is designed to make almost impossible. Almost.

Kerr’s everyday spine that year featured Curry, Klay, Draymond, Iguodala, and Barnes: shooting gravity, switching defense, and two-way versatility that still defines the target. Your projected win percentage is how often your lineup clears that wall across an 82-game sample.

## The best squad you could have signed

When the sim ends, the engine looks back at the five team-seasons you landed on and computes the strongest legal five under the same $100M cap. Identical wheel luck, perfect decisions.

The gap is usually a few points per game—a star you passed on for value, or a cheaper wing that should have funded a bigger swing. Learning to read that gap is how you climb from lottery vibes to a real contender.

## Why 73-9 still matters

In 2015-16 the Warriors went 73-9, edging past the 1995-96 Bulls’ 72-10 for the best regular-season mark the league has recorded. They then lost the Finals. The debate over whether that team crowns the greatest regular-season club will never fully settle.

This challenge keeps the argument on a scoreboard: pull legends from thirty years of team-seasons, throw them at that historic core, and see what holds up across eighty-two games.
